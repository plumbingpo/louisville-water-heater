Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Hillview

A little routine care is the difference between a water heater that lasts and one that fails early. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: an annual sediment flush, an anode rod check every three to five years, a quick relief valve test once a year, a temperature setting around 120 degrees, and annual descaling for tankless units on hard water, so small issues get caught early.

Equipment age matters just as much. With regular maintenance, a tank water heater usually runs 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like discolored hot water, popping noises, or a damp spot around the base, it's smart to start planning a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule is cheaper and is far less stressful than an emergency swap with water spreading across the floor.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to high water pressure or thermal expansion with nowhere to go, both modest fixes. The same drip ignored for months can wear out the valve and stress the tank until the tank itself fails, and a small repair becomes a full replacement and a cleanup. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.
