How Shively Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

A little routine care is the difference between a water heater that runs for years and one that floods the garage in year seven. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: an annual sediment flush, an anode inspection every few years, a yearly test of the T&P valve, a thermostat set near 120 degrees, and annual descaling for tankless units on hard water, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

The age of your heater matters just as much. With regular maintenance, a standard tank usually runs 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like rust-colored water, rumbling, or a damp spot around the base, it's time to plan a replacement. A planned replacement is cheaper and is far less stressful than scrambling for a new heater with water spreading across the floor.

The earlier you report a hot water problem, the cheaper it usually is. A tank that's just begun to rumble needs only a flush, yet the same sediment, ignored through a few more years, can overheat the bottom of the tank and burn out the lower element on an electric model, turning a simple visit into a replacement. Save our number on your phone now so help is one call away as soon as something sounds or looks wrong. A quick call saves time, money, and ruined floors.
