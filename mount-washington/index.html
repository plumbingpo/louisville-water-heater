How Mount Washington Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

Routine maintenance makes all the difference between a water heater that lasts and one that fails early. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: an annual sediment flush, an anode inspection every few years, a yearly test of the T&P valve, a temperature setting around 120 degrees, and annual descaling for tankless units on hard water, so small issues get caught early.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters last 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models usually run 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing symptoms such as discolored hot water, popping and rumbling, moisture around the base, or hot water that runs out sooner than it used to, a planned replacement is almost always cheaper and much easier on you than an emergency one after the tank lets go.

The sooner you call about a hot water problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A sediment layer that's just starting to rumble can often be fixed with a flush, yet the same sediment, neglected for a few years, can create hot spots on the tank floor and burn out the lower element on an electric model, forcing a replacement much sooner. Save our number on your phone today so help is one call away the moment something seems off. A quick call saves time, money, and ruined floors.

Common Water Heater Problems in Mount Washington

Every type of water heater fails in its own way, and we work on all of them in Mount Washington. Gas tanks tend to have trouble with pilot assemblies and thermocouples, dirty burners, and venting that doesn't draft. Electric units lose heating elements and thermostats, especially when sediment piles up around the lower element. Tankless units show error codes for scale buildup, ignition faults, and flow sensor trouble, and many of those trace back to skipped descaling. Hybrid heat pump units depend on a clean filter and a free-flowing condensate line, and they struggle in cramped closets. Our technicians work on all four every week and stock the common parts to finish most repairs in one trip.
