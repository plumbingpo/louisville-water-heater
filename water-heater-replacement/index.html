Most tank water heaters in Louisville, KY last somewhere between eight and twelve years, and that number drops with hard water, high pressure, or a tank nobody ever flushed. Most tanks give a few warnings near the end: water seeping from under the jacket, rust in the hot water, sediment noise that gets louder by the month, and showers that go cold sooner every winter. A leak from the tank body is the clearest signal of all. When the glass lining gives out and the steel corrodes through, there is no part to replace, and a slow weep can turn into a flooded floor with no notice.

How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • A second or third repair within a year or two
  • Operating costs climbing on an older, less efficient heater
  • Water leaking from the bottom or body of the tank
  • Rust flaking from the tank jacket or its connections
  • Loud rumbling or popping that flushing no longer fixes
  • Rusty or brown water from the hot side only
  • A water heater that is more than about ten years old
  • Hot water running out faster every month

Small Symptoms, Big Consequences

Most water heater failures start small: a popping sound from sediment, a damp spot under the drain valve, or a shower that goes lukewarm a few minutes sooner each week. If you notice any of these signs, contact Louisville Water Heater at (502) 205-4594 for a prompt inspection.

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

Swapping a water heater looks simple until you count the connections. Gas units need a leak-free connection with a sediment trap, a correctly sized and pitched vent that sends carbon monoxide outdoors, and enough combustion air. An electric unit needs a circuit and breaker matched to the element wattage. Every tank needs a correctly rated relief valve with a discharge pipe routed to a safe spot, and many homes also need an expansion tank, a drain pan, or seismic straps under local code. Going tankless or switching to a heat pump adds even more: bigger gas lines, different vent materials, dedicated circuits, and condensate drains.

Professional replacement gets all of it right the first time. Our experienced technicians size the heater to your household, verify the gas line, vent, and electrical service, and install whatever code items apply. We leak-test, check draft and carbon monoxide on gas units, and set a safe temperature before we leave. It is what separates a heater that runs quietly for years from one that has problems from the start. And if a repair would buy you several good years on your current tank, we tell you that honestly instead of selling you a new one.

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Make sure an expansion tank is in place if your home has a closed plumbing system
  • Set the thermostat around 120 degrees to prevent scalding and reduce wear
  • Flush the new tank once or twice a year to keep sediment from building up
  • Have the anode rod checked around year three to five and replaced if it is worn
  • Heat pump units need a clean filter and open airflow to run efficiently
  • Plan ahead: knowing the heater's age lets you replace it on your schedule, not after a leak
  • Descale a tankless unit every year, or more often with hard water
